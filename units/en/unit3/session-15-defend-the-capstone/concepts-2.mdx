# Reading your own trace under pressure [[reading-traces]]

## Why the trace is the whole defence

Six minutes is not long enough to reason from first principles about what just
went wrong. It is long enough to read a trace out loud, if the trace says
anything.

That is what session 9 was for. An event carries what was attempted, with what
arguments, what came back, and how long it took. A trace with a gap where a
tool call should be is worse than no trace: it reads as though nothing happened
there, and you will say so, and you will be wrong.

## The diagnosis, in order

When the injected failure lands, resist explaining. Read instead.

1. **Where did it stop?** The last event, and whether it is an ending the
   system chose or one it fell into.
2. **What was the last thing it believed?** The retrieved documents, the
   arguments to the last tool call.
3. **Which contract broke?** Every session gave you one: a schema that rejects,
   a tool that refuses, a budget that ends a loop, a transition that is not
   declared. Name the one that should have caught it.
4. **Did it fail safely?** A readable refusal is a pass. A traceback on screen
   is a finding, and saying so plainly is better than talking over it.
