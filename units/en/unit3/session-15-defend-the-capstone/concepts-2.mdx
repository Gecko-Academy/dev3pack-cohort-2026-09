# Reading your buyer under pressure [[reading-the-run]]

## Why the output is the whole defence

Six minutes is not long enough to reason from first principles about what just went
wrong. It is long enough to read the buyer's output out loud, line by line, because every
line says which step it came from:

```
  [  ok] pin       1 x 'Espresso', budget 2000000, mint Eoqdd43n..  -> intents/...
  [  ok] prepare   simulated: pass, expires in 150 blocks
  [  NO] check     REFUSED on quantity: asked 2, prepared 1
  Nothing was signed.
```

## The diagnosis, in order

When the card lands, resist explaining. Read instead.

1. **Where did it stop?** The step with `NO` or `STOP`, and whether it is a refusal the
   buyer chose or a failure it fell into.
2. **Which field decided?** A refusal names one field and both values: what was asked,
   from the pin, and what was found, in the bytes or from verify or the signer.
3. **Was anything signed or sent?** The last line says `Nothing was signed` or
   `Nothing was submitted`. That line is the pass.
4. **Where is it written down?** `refusals/<stamp>-<field>.json`. Open it: it is the
   evidence that outlives the screen.

`STOP` is different from `NO`. `STOP` means the buyer could not reach what a step needed:
the network, Gecko, or a file. Say so, and switch to the recorded lane with
`GECKO_SOURCE=recorded`. Nothing was signed either way.

## Rehearse the card, not the demo

Run all four before Friday: `uv run buyer --cards --recorded` offline, then each one live
on devnet. You will find at least one you did not expect. That is the point of finding it
on Thursday rather than on stage.

## The rubric

Six areas, each scored 0 to 3 from what you show and what is in your repository.

| Area | 3 looks like |
|---|---|
| Environment and assistant workflow | `store/store.json` and your devnet store; one command per lane; your assistant connected; no key anywhere in the repository |
| Grounding and tool use | every pin in `intents/` written before its prepare; a receipt with the explorer link, the ledger deltas and `total_purchases` n to n+1 |
| Reliability and evaluation | at least four refusals in `refusals/`, each naming the field and both values; the card refused live; `docs/EVAL_REPORT.md` with the five cases |
| Python foundations | tests that trigger every refusal offline, green; lint clean |
| Skills and MCP integration | `verify_signed_transaction` before every submit; your check served as an MCP server with its guard |
| Capstone explanation | the six minutes on time; the ADR with what would reverse it; one real incident in `docs/ISSUES.md`, said before anyone asks |

**The minimum defence** passes with projects 01 and 02 offline and one refusal explained
well. Presenting from the recorded lane after a network failure is not penalised when you
say that is what you are doing.
