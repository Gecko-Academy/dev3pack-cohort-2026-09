# Defend the capstone

**Friday, October 2, 2026 · 2h**

## Outcome

You present the capstone, and then you are handed one failure you did not
prepare for. You diagnose it from your own traces, and either recover or fail
safely in front of the room. A demo that only shows the happy path proves that
the happy path exists, which nobody doubted.

## Contract and threat boundary

| | |
|---|---|
| Input | Your capstone, running, with its traces on. Six minutes, hard-timed. |
| Output | A demo, an architecture decision you can defend, measured evidence, and one recovery. |
| Budget | Six minutes each. The clock is the constraint the format is built around, so rehearse against it. |
| Failure to handle | One injected failure, drawn at the start of your slot. You do not know which. |

The threat here is not a hostile question. It is the demo that works because it
was run four times before the room saw it, and answers nothing about whether it
holds when something breaks.

## The six minutes

| Minute | What |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The problem, and who has it |
| 2–3 | The demo: one supported answer with citations, one refusal |
| 4 | The architecture decision you would defend, and what would reverse it |
| 5 | Measured evidence: your evaluation report, not an impression |
| 6 | **The injected failure**, diagnosed from your traces |

## The injected failure

At the start of your slot you draw one of these. They are all conditions your
agent will meet in the wild, and every one of them appeared in a session.

| Failure | Where you met it |
|---|---|
| The retrieval returns nothing | Session 6 |
| A citation supports nothing it claims | Session 7 |
| A tool times out mid-loop | Sessions 2 and 5 |
| Retrieved text carries an instruction | Sessions 4 and 13 |
| The same call repeats until the budget ends it | Session 5 |
| The state machine is asked for an illegal move | Session 8 |

Recovery is not the only pass. Failing safely is a pass: a refusal the caller
can read, a trace that shows where it stopped, and an accurate sentence about
what you would fix. Claiming it worked when the trace says otherwise is the one
answer that fails.

## What you are judged on

The [assessment rubric](../../unit2/capstone/concepts-3.mdx), on
evidence rather than polish. Your notebook scorecards are formative and are not
the grade; what they are good for is finding the gap you would rather not meet
on stage. Run them the night before.

The [final assignment](../../tracks/final-assignment/introduction.mdx) is scored
separately, against a private question set, and its certificate needs both the
aggregate bar and every critical safety case.

## Before you present

```bash
uv run bootcamp progress            # every scorecard, in one table
uv run bootcamp check cap01         # the capstone's own five checks
```

Bring the totals. Bring your traces. Bring the one limitation you would name
before anyone asks, because naming it first is the difference between a
limitation and a hole somebody found.
