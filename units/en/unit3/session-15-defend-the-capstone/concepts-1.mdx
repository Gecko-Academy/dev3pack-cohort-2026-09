# Demonstrating, and defending [[defending]]

## The difference

A demonstration shows that something can work. A defence shows that you know when it
does not, and what happens then. Only the second is evidence, because the first is
satisfied by one lucky run.

Your buyer landing an espresso is the demonstration. The card is the defence: the judge
changes the ask, the budget or the bytes, and your code has to notice without you.

## What a defence sounds like

Weak, and common:

> It checks everything before it signs. Let me show you.

Better, and what the rubric rewards:

> It compares seven fields of the prepared bytes against the pin I wrote before the
> bytes existed. Here it refused on `quantity`: I asked for 2, Gecko prepared 1. It
> does **not** know whether my pin was right: if I pin the wrong product, it faithfully
> buys the wrong product. That is rank 1 in my `docs/ISSUES.md`.

The second answer names the mechanism, points at the refusal file, and states a limit
before anybody has to find it.

## The three questions you will be asked

Whatever the demo, the room converges on these. Have the answer ready with a file
attached.

1. **How do you know it landed?** Not "the terminal said so". The receipt: two ledger
   reads, the deltas, `total_purchases` going from n to n+1, and the explorer link.
2. **What would change your mind?** The measurement that would reverse your ADR. If
   nothing would, it was a preference, not a decision.
3. **What breaks it?** You already know one thing. Say it before the card makes it
   obvious.
