# Everything that is graded, in one place

Two kinds of grading, and they never mix:

| | The track | The final assignment |
|---|---|---|
| What | **15 marked items**: sessions 1 to 14, and the `cap01` notebook | your research agent, on 15 private questions |
| Worth | **5,100 marks** in all | a pass or not, and the certificate |
| Where you see it | [the track](https://github.com/Gecko-Academy/dev3pack-submissions/blob/main/TRACK.md), one row per student | `dev3pack-submissions/finals/<you>/result.json` |
| How you hand it in | `uv run bootcamp submit <item> --github <you> --push`, from your course folder | `uv run bootcamp final submit --github <you>`, from `my-final-assignment` |

The Gecko capstone, your store and buyer, is neither: it is judged from your own
`my-gecko-buyer`, and you hand in **a link** to it by 5 October. Session 15 has no
notebook. Both deadlines, step by step: [hand in by 5 October](hand-in-by-october-5.mdx).

## The 15 marked items

Every item is handed in the same way, from your course folder, after `git pull` and
`uv sync --extra projects --extra agents`:

```bash
uv run bootcamp check ch07                         # see what passes, before you hand in
uv run bootcamp submit ch07 --github <you> --push  # hand it in
```

Replace `ch07` with the item. **Save the notebook first**: `submit` reads the file as
saved on disk. You can hand an item in again at any time.

## The three that need more than the notebook

**The weekly challenges (`ch05` and `ch10`, up to 500 each).** You build the challenge in
its demo notebook: [demo 8](https://github.com/Gecko-Academy/dev3pack-cohort-2026-09/blob/main/demos/08_the_weekly_challenge.ipynb) for week 1,
[demo 10](https://github.com/Gecko-Academy/dev3pack-cohort-2026-09/blob/main/demos/10_your_store_and_buyer.ipynb) for week 2. Save it after the line
`week 1 challenge: N/500` (or `week 2 ...`) has printed. Then
`uv run bootcamp submit ch05 --github <you> --push` carries the saved demo with your
hand-in, and the track reads your points from that line. The demo as shipped earns
nothing: the points need code that is yours.

**`cap01`, the final assignment's notebook (500).** It lives in the course folder, under
unit 2. It is scored on the track like a session. It is **not** the final assignment's
grade: that is the 15 private questions below.
[The final assignment tutorial](final-assignment.mdx) shows how `cap01` connects to
your repository.

**`ch01`, session 1 (100).** It changes settings on your own machine, so the course
cannot re-run it for you. Hand in the notebook as it says; the track counts the
hand-in.

## Reading the track

Your row on [the track](https://github.com/Gecko-Academy/dev3pack-submissions/blob/main/TRACK.md)
shows each item as `earned/possible`. An empty cell means nothing was handed in yet,
which is not the same as a zero. `200/700` on `ch05` or `ch10` means the session is
done and the challenge is not. **5,100/5,100 is every item, including both challenges.**

A pull request that is green merges on its own, and the track updates after the merge.
No checks, or it never merges? [Handing work in](../unit0/how-to-submit.mdx) has what to
do.

## The final assignment: graded separately

Yes, it is graded, and it is the one that earns the certificate. It is not a column on
the track.

- **What:** your research agent answers 15 private questions, 6 of them critical.
- **Pass:** a score of at least 30% **and** every critical question passed.
- **Hand in:** from `my-final-assignment`, `uv run bootcamp final submit --github <you>`.
  Rehearse first with `--dry-run`.
- **Your result:** `dev3pack-submissions/finals/<you>/result.json`, a few minutes after
  the pull request merges. The latest submission counts.

Every step: [the final assignment tutorial](final-assignment.mdx).

## Share it

Your work is public on purpose, so it is worth showing:

- **Your track row.** The track is public: link it and point at your row. Each score
  sits next to the notebook that earned it, in `submissions/<you>/`.
- **Your final assignment repository.** It is your showcase: its README shows a demo, your
  measured results and the command that produced them
  ([section 12 of the tutorial](final-assignment.mdx)).
- **Your certificate**, once the course issues it. It is signed, and anyone can verify
  it: [the final assignment page](../tracks/final-assignment/introduction.mdx) shows how.
  Add it to your LinkedIn profile under *Licenses and certifications*, and post it with
  the link to your repository.
- **Your capstone presentation.** A two-minute clip of your buyer refusing the judge's
  card, with the explorer link to your devnet receipt, says more than any badge.
